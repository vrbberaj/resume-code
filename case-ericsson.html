01 • Business Context & Stakes

The Enterprise Crisis: Why Legacy Processes Were Failing

At Ericsson Global, over 1,200 technical sales engineers across 45 countries were configuring complex 5G network infrastructure deployments spanning thousands of line items. However, the entire quoting machinery relied on a monolithic, unversioned 50MB Microsoft Excel workbook containing 14 dense tabs, fragile Visual Basic macros, and manual regional rate cards.

Whenever a sales engineer modified a discount cell, broken #REF! formulas frequently cascaded across interdependent hardware calculations. Worse still, unauthorized configuration overrides slipped through unnoticed, causing severe pricing discrepancies, delivery friction, and unapproved discount discrepancies across global markets. Quotes crawled through a 6-day email bottleneck waiting for Commercial Directors to manually verify Excel cell formulas.

Strategic Design Leadership Mandate
Architect an enterprise CPQ cloud application that eliminated spreadsheet calculation errors and enforced automated pricing validation guardrails, while strictly matching the keyboard data-entry velocity that senior electrical engineers demanded.
02 • In-Situ Field Research & Mental Models

Contextual Inquiries & Empathy Mapping

To understand why prior corporate software rollouts failed with less than 15% adoption, I traveled to Stockholm, Sweden to conduct contextual inquiry sessions shadowing telecom sales engineers on live customer bids. I discovered that engineers didn’t love Excel for its visual interface—they loved it for keyboard muscle memory (arrow keys, copy-paste across cells, instantaneous autocomplete). Corporate web wizards with dropdowns and multi-step dialogs felt painfully slow and restrictive.

07 • Hard Business ROI & Leadership CAR Defense

Leadership Interview Defense Q&A (Situation, Action, Result)

Direct leadership defenses addressing critical operational tensions between sales velocity and commercial governance.

Q1 Sales engineers are notoriously protective of their Excel spreadsheets. How did you overcome their intense resistance to a new web tool?
Context / Situation

Previous corporate attempts to replace the 14-tab Excel sheet with heavy web wizards failed with under 15% adoption because sales engineers felt slow, handcuffed, and restricted by multi-step dropdown forms.

UX Leadership Action

During contextual inquiry in Stockholm, I discovered engineers loved Excel not for its aesthetics, but for keyboard velocity. I scrapped the wizard paradigm and architected a high-speed data grid featuring arrow-key cell navigation, tabular copy-paste, and inline search. We preserved their muscle memory while silently moving the catalog and pricing validation to the cloud.

Business Result

Adoption exceeded 94% within the first month. Quote turnaround dropped from 6 days to 42 minutes, and the project won 1st Place in the Ericsson Global Hack-fest Optimization Contest.

Q2 What was the biggest tension between Sales and Commercial Governance, and how did your UX architecture resolve it?
Context / Situation

Sales demanded total quoting flexibility to respond quickly to complex tenders, while Commercial Governance and Pricing teams needed strict guardrails to prevent unapproved discount tiers and invalid hardware configurations.

UX Leadership Action

I designed dynamic, three-tiered validation guardrails into the data grid. Standard configurations remained in a high-speed Green Channel for instant export. Out-of-bounds configurations triggered clear, non-blocking inline feedback with automated escalation workflows, eliminating subjective email disputes.

Business Result

Compressed deal approval turnaround by 92%, enforced 100% catalog rule compliance across 14 global market units, and completely eliminated broken formula errors.
