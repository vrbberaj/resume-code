User Needs & Calibrating Mental Models

The fundamental breakdown in modern enterprise AI applications stems from an unexamined cognitive gap: deterministic expectations applied to probabilistic machinery. In traditional software, clicking a button executes a mathematical certainty—if the outcome deviates, the software is broken. In machine learning systems, outputs are statistical approximations generated across multidimensional vector spaces.

“If users don’t understand how an AI system works or what its boundaries are, they will inevitably form an inaccurate mental model. When the model fails, the drop in trust is catastrophic.” — Google People + AI Research (PAIR) Guidebook

Google PAIR identifies the Calibration Curve as the central design challenge of AI onboarding. The goal is neither to induce blind worship of the AI nor to trigger dismissive skepticism, but to establish calibrated trust where the operator understands precisely when to delegate and when to verify.

Mental Model State Observed User Behavior Executive Design Remedy (PAIR)
Under-Trust
High Friction		 Operators reject automated assistance; double-check routine database rows manually; treat the AI as an annoyance. Surface small, highly reliable micro-wins early. Provide transparent telemetry on data freshness and training sources.
Calibrated Trust
Optimal Equilibrium ⭐		 Operators automate 85% of repetitive tasks; scrutinize high-blast-radius anomalies; understand edge cases. The system clearly communicates confidence thresholds and explicitly signals when an inquiry falls outside its core capability envelope.
Over-Trust
Legal & Compliance Risk		 Operators rubber-stamp AI-generated proposals without reading; assume the model is omniscient. Introduce deliberate "speed bumps" and forced verification checkpoints on high-liability transactions (e.g. wire transfers, fleet dispatches).
PAIR Guideline: The Co-Planning Onboarding Framework PAIR: Needs & Expectations §2

Never present enterprise users with an empty, unconstrained chat box. Google PAIR demonstrates that conversational text prompts create boundless expectations that modern models cannot fulfill. Instead, anchor the initial interface with scoped intent chips that explicitly demarcate:

1. What the agent can execute autonomously: (e.g., “Sync pending invoices under $5,000 across NetSuite and SAP”).
2. What requires human oversight: (e.g., “Customer refunds above standard warranty thresholds require tier-2 manager sign-off”).
3. The data lineage: (e.g., “Trained on production telemetry through October 2026”).

Explainability, Confidence Telemetry & The EU AI Act

In enterprise product design, explainability is not a visual gimmick—it is a legal and operational mandate. Under Article 13 of the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act), high-risk enterprise software must be engineered to ensure that operations are sufficiently transparent to enable users to interpret system outputs and use them appropriately.

Both Google PAIR and Microsoft HAX divide explainability into two distinct operational paradigms:

1. Global Explainability
Describes the system's overarching algorithmic rationale. Answers: “How does this credit dispute model evaluate transactions in general?” Belongs in system settings, documentation, and compliance audit reports.
2. Local Explainability
Explains the reasoning behind a single, isolated prediction. Answers: “Why did the system flag THIS particular shipment from Antwerp?” Must be surfaced inline, at the exact point of decision.

Handling Hallucinations & The 18 HAX Guidelines

In 2019, Microsoft Research published the definitive taxonomy for human-AI interaction based on evaluations across 20+ commercial AI products. The core premise: all AI systems will fail, drift, and hallucinate. Elite product design does not pretend failure is impossible; it designs friction-free recovery mechanisms into the core interaction loops.

HAX Rule Guideline Name Operational Enterprise UX Execution
G7 Support Efficient Invocation Make it easy to invoke AI capabilities when needed without breaking user flow (e.g., keyboard shortcuts, contextual command palettes like ⌘K).
G8 Support Efficient Dismissal Make it easy to dismiss or ignore unneeded AI suggestions with zero friction (single click or ESC key). Never force a confirmation modal just to close an AI tooltip.
G9 Support Efficient Correction ⭐ When the model makes an error, editing the output must require fewer keystrokes than retyping the entire field manually. Provide inline diffing and granular parameter adjustments.
G10 Scope to Context Tailor AI actions to current user intent and spatial focus. Never interrupt a high-stakes manual task with speculative automations.
G11 Acknowledge & Degrade Gracefully When confidence drops or an inference fails, degrade seamlessly to deterministic fallback UI (e.g., manual search filters) without throwing opaque 500 error modals.
G13 Learn from User Behaviors When an operator repeatedly edits a specific parameter, the system captures that delta as an implicit feedback signal to calibrate future thresholds.
“Guideline 9 is where most enterprise software fails. If an operator has to delete an entire hallucinated paragraph and retype it from scratch, the perceived cognitive cost of using the AI exceeds the benefit.” — Microsoft Human-AI eXperience (HAX) Research
Chapter 04 • Source: IBM Carbon for AI

Enterprise Agentic UX: The Death of the Pure Chat Interface

The conversational chat bubble (the “ChatGPT pattern”) is an excellent discovery medium for consumers, but an operational catastrophe for complex enterprise software. In enterprise domains—such as telecom provisioning, fleet telemetry, or financial auditing—operators manage structured, multi-entity relationships. Expecting a professional user to scroll through 40 vertical chat bubbles to audit a database batch update violates every principle of Information Architecture.

IBM Carbon for AI establishes the modern standard: the Split-Pane Co-Pilot Architecture.

IBM Carbon: The Dual-Surface Paradigm Carbon Design System AI Spec

Left Rail (The Conversational Orchestrator): Serves as the natural-language steering wheel. The operator specifies intent, receives multi-turn status updates, and asks clarifying questions.

Right Main Stage (The Interactive Canvas): Rather than dumping unformatted markdown text, the system renders structured Carbon action cards, live data tables, and direct-manipulation controls.

The Human Approval Barrier: Before any state-altering transaction is written to the enterprise ledger, the agent halts and surfaces a dedicated review card displaying:
• Target Entities Affected (Blast Radius)
• Before vs. After Delta Comparison
• One-Click Approval / Rejection Controls

Chapter 05 • Executive Interview Playbook

Director & VP Interview Defense Scripts

When interviewing for Director of Product Design, Head of UX, or Senior Design Manager roles, executive hiring committees (VPs of Product, CPOs, and Engineering Directors) do not ask you how to make Figma auto-layouts. They grill you on technical risk, cross-functional conflict, regulatory compliance, and business ROI.

Here are 3 hard executive interview scenarios with word-for-word scripts synthesized from real FAANG, Tier-1 tech, and EU regulatory rubrics:

Hard Scenario 01 • Technical Conflict
“What do you do when your ML engineering leads want to ship a 70% accuracy model into production, but your design team argues it’s too inaccurate and will damage user trust?”
“I don’t treat model accuracy as a binary design blocker; I treat it as an interaction design problem. A 70% model cannot have autonomous write-access to production databases, but it can provide immense productivity if framed properly.

Following Microsoft HAX Guideline G2 and Google PAIR, we decouple the model from execution and introduce progressive confidence gating: at 70%, the AI acts strictly as an assistive recommender. We design the interface with clear local explainability and one-click human verification, ensuring that the 30% error rate creates zero customer blast radius. We define measurable guardrails: if human override frequency exceeds 25%, we automatically dial back automation. This protects delivery timelines and engineering momentum while safeguarding user trust.”
Hard Scenario 02 • European Compliance & Trust
“How do you architect explainability for enterprise platforms under the EU AI Act without overwhelming non-technical business users?”
“We separate mathematical complexity from operational utility. Enterprise operators do not need to inspect vector weights; they need causal lineage and confidence telemetry.

I implement a three-tiered progressive disclosure model compliant with Articles 13 and 14 of the EU AI Act:
1. Inline Glance: An ambient confidence badge showing the driving causal factor (e.g., ‘Flagged due to unusual shipping velocity’).
2. Hover Telemetry: Direct citation chips linking to the exact source documents and transaction logs retrieved via RAG.
3. Audit Drawer: An immutable historical ledger comparing the decision against historical peer baselines for compliance teams. This allows rapid operator workflows while satisfying strict European regulatory auditability.”
Hard Scenario 03 • Organizational Leadership
“How do you lead and modernize a design organization of 15+ designers in the era of AI and design systems?”
“AI design leadership operates on two simultaneous tracks: what we build and how we work.

For what we build, I institutionalize system-wide governance across our design tokens and component libraries—leveraging frameworks like IBM Carbon for AI to ensure consistency across squads.

For how we work, we modernize internal DesignOps by integrating AI into research synthesis, automated design token auditing, and synthetic user telemetry. This compresses research turnaround times by 40%, freeing senior product designers from repetitive wireframing to focus on high-leverage systems architecture and business differentiation.”
