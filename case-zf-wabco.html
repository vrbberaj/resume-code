01 • Business Context & Stakes

The Enterprise Crisis: Why Legacy Processes Were Failing

Operating a 40-ton commercial articulated truck on high-speed European motorways at night presents severe cognitive overload. Aftermarket navigation tablets demanded repeated touch taps, bright backlight glare compromised night vision, and eyes-off-the-road times frequently exceeded 2.5 seconds—violating strict European Union Commercial Vehicle Safety Directives.

Strategic Design Leadership Mandate
Design an ergonomic in-cab digital instrument cluster cluster that presents mission-critical speed, braking radar, and turn guidance within strict <1.2-second glance cones, paired with real-time CAN-bus fleet radar for dispatchers.
02 • In-Situ Field Research & Mental Models

Contextual Inquiries & Empathy Mapping

Conducting in-cab contextual inquiries with long-haul drivers in Germany, we identified intense environmental challenges: violent road vibration at 90 km/h, rapid shifts in ambient light (entering tunnels), and cognitive fatigue during 9-hour shifts. Drivers demanded high-contrast typography and zero tiny touch interaction targets.

07 • Hard Business ROI & Leadership CAR Defense

Leadership Interview Defense Q&A (Situation, Action, Result)

Defense examining automotive ergonomics, human factors engineering, and mission-critical safety compliance.

Q1 How did European automotive safety regulations govern your user experience decisions for the in-cab display?
Context / Situation

EU commercial transport regulations strictly prohibit displays demanding more than 1.2 seconds of eyes-off-road glance time to avoid high-speed collision risks.

UX Leadership Action

I partitioned the display into 3 glance zones. Critical vehicle speed and radar distance occupied Zone 1 directly in front of the driver. Navigation turns occupied Zone 2 with large, high-contrast turn arrows. Eco-coaching was relegated to subtle green peripheral pulses. We eliminated all sub-menus and touch requirements while driving.

Business Result

Average driver glance time clocked at 0.94s (comfortably below the 1.2s legal ceiling), cutting critical navigation errors by 18% across 80,000 deployed European vehicles.
