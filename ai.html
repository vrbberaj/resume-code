Part 01 • UX for AI (Designing AI Products)

Designing AI is much bigger than putting a chat box on a screen

Good AI UX starts before anyone writes a prompt. As a design leader, my job is first to stop teams from adding AI where a simple rule or filter works better—and then to design every part of the AI experience around real human habits, speed, clarity, and control.

Why Most AI Features Get Ignored

Empty Chat Boxes & Guesswork

  • Using slow, unpredictable AI where a simple button, search filter, or rule is faster and 100% reliable.
  • Dropping people in front of an empty prompt box when they don’t know what or how to ask.
  • Forcing users to regenerate an entire answer from scratch just to fix one wrong sentence.
  • Acting overconfident without showing sources—so checking the AI takes longer than doing the work by hand.
How My Teams Design AI

Embedded, Steerable & Easy to Check

  • Save AI for messy work—summarizing, spotting patterns, and first drafts—while keeping exact workflows deterministic.
  • Move beyond chat with inline canvas actions, smart templates, or voice and scan input when hands are busy.
  • Make output easy to check and edit inline, with clear sources, honest progress, and one-click undo.
  • Partner with PM and data teams early to define what a “good” AI answer looks like and how to test it.
Complete Product Lens • Beyond Just Trust

10 things I focus on when designing AI products

Trust matters, but it is only one piece of the job. Here is the full 10-part playbook I use with designers, product managers, and engineers when taking an AI feature from idea to production:

01

Deciding if it even needs AI

Know when not to use AI. Save AI for unstructured synthesis, pattern spotting, and drafting—never replace a fast, reliable rule or filter with a slow model.

In my work: Kept deterministic safety rules in fleet & telecom flows; reserved AI for complex SysML drafting and threat triage.
02

Setting clear expectations

Help people understand upfront what the AI can and can’t do, so they neither over-rely on it blindly nor give up on it after one imperfect answer.

In my work: Framed IBM Rhapsody AI clearly as a first-pass model drafter for engineers to review, not an auto-publish black box.
03

Better ways to ask (beyond the prompt box)

Move past empty text boxes using smart suggestions, structured inputs, direct canvas actions, and voice or camera scan when a user’s hands are busy.

In my work: Direct diagram generation on the Rhapsody canvas, and voice/barcode workflows on Zebra warehouse devices.
04

Deciding how much the AI does on its own

Define clear boundaries for when the AI only suggests, when it prepares a draft for approval, and when it acts by itself—especially as agents take on multi-step tasks.

In my work: Shipped a 3-tier autonomy model across IBM security and engineering products (see interactive example below).
05

Making output easy to check & edit

Show clear sources and confidence, and let people edit one section inline so verifying a draft is five times faster than redoing the work from scratch.

In my work: Clickable source links and side-by-side diffs cut SysML drafting time by 74% and raised SUS from 61 to 89.
06

Handling mistakes gracefully

Design for the moments AI gets it wrong—with one-click undo, easy correction, staged rollbacks, and failing gracefully into a manual screen instead of failing silently.

In my work: When confidence drops in MaaS360, AI hands over everything it already parsed so admins never start from a blank page.
07

Waiting, speed & responsiveness

Stream useful answers progressively, show honest step-by-step progress on multi-step agent tasks, and let people keep working instead of staring at a spinner.

In my work: Live agent step trackers and safe mid-run cancel controls for long security and compliance scans.
08

Learning from natural feedback

Use people’s real corrections, approvals, and edits to improve the AI over time—without interrupting their flow with constant rating popups.

In my work: Replaced 6 separate feedback popups at IBM with one quiet signal captured naturally when operators edit a draft.
09

Memory, privacy, fairness & regulation

Let people see and control what context the AI remembers about them, while designing directly for accessibility, bias checks, GDPR, and EU AI Act transparency rules.

In my work: Enforced GDPR privacy-by-design, WCAG 2.2 AA, and EU AI Act transparency checks across global enterprise releases.
10

Measuring AI quality alongside PM & data

Designers help define what a “good” AI answer actually looks like and how to test it—measuring real task completion and how much editing users had to do, not just clicks.

In my work: Partnered with PM and AI engineers on quality rubrics, acceptance rates, and time-to-verified-outcome before GA launch.

Part 02 • AI for UX (Team Velocity & DesignOps)

How I bring AI into the team’s own design workflow

Individual AI speed only matters if an entire team can use it safely. Across a 14-person group spanning UI/UX design, UX research, and content, I standardized how we synthesize research, stress-test edge cases, and prototype in live code.

LIVE WORKFLOW WALKTHROUGH
Phase 1 · Privacy-Safe Discovery Synthesis

Redacted Enterprise Logs → Exhaustive Failure Matrix

We never paste raw customer PII into external models. Using redacted field transcripts and domain SLA rules inside enterprise-governed LLMs, we surface rare failure states before touching Figma.

[Input] 18 anonymized operator transcripts + SLA compliance rules
[Output] 14 non-happy-path states mapped (stale telemetry, partial rollback, permission conflict)
[Impact] Discovery synthesis compressed from 8 days → 3 hours
Phase 2 · Interactive Code Prototyping

Static Flows → Live Token-Constrained HTML/React Sandbox

Instead of debating flat wireframes, we generate interactive code prototypes strictly bound to existing Design System tokens—testing real keyboard density, latency skeletons, and human override diffs on Day 2.

[Constraint] Carbon v11 semantic tokens + WCAG 2.2 AA contrast locked
[Prototype] Live interactive blast-radius diff & bulk keyboard triage
[Impact] Validated with Principal Engineers in Sprint Zero; UI rework cut by 70%
Phase 3 · 14-Person Team Multiplier

Personal Workflow → Repeatable Team Playbook

Codified shared prompt libraries, PII redaction rules, content-linting checks, and our 10-point AI UX rubric across UI/UX designers, UX researchers, and content specialists.

[Governance] Zero raw customer PII in external models • Mandatory human critique
[Adoption] 14 specialists upskilled via weekly AI-UX studio pairing
[Outcome] End-to-end feature cycle time cut from 6 weeks → under 2 weeks
Stack: IBM watsonx • Copilot • ChatGPT Enterprise • NotebookLM 6 wks → <2 wks cycle

1. Faster Research Without Privacy Risk

Strict PII redaction first—then AI synthesis across interview transcripts and support tickets to uncover edge cases early.

2. Live Prototypes Over Static Deck Debates

Putting working, token-compliant interactions in front of users and engineers in days so we agree on behavior before sprint commitment.

3. AI Assists Craft — Humans Own Decisions

AI accelerates first-pass synthesis, microcopy checks, and state coverage, while designers and researchers own problem framing and user judgment.
