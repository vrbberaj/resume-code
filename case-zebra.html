Device health insights for rugged enterprise fleets

IT managers running thousands of Zebra handhelds couldn’t see why devices failed. I led the design team behind VisibilityIQ device-health dashboards, and built the team while doing it.

01

The problem

Situation

Enterprise IT managers like “Jamie” (our main persona) had battery, application and memory data spread across separate reports. They found out about failing devices when workers complained.

Customers such as Australia Post asked for things we couldn’t show: which app was draining the battery, and which devices needed repair before the shift.

On the warehouse floor, workers in freezers wore thick gloves that made standard touch targets unusable.

05

Key decisions

The calls I made and what they cost

1

Organise by what IT managers act on

Why: The card sort showed people think in Devices, Applications and Battery, not in our data sources.

Trade-off: Engineering had to join data from three back-ends into one view.

2

Every insight ends in an action

Why: Customers didn’t want charts; they wanted “Request repair” or “Request OS update”.

Trade-off: Each action needed a back-end workflow, so we launched with four and added more later.

3

Glove-friendly handheld UI

Why: Freezer visits showed 44px targets failed with thermal gloves. We moved to 72px with high contrast.

Trade-off: Fewer items per screen, so flows got one step longer.

06

Results

Business and user impact

Customers called the new dashboards “clearer, much less crowded”. Pairing failures dropped 84%, staging throughput rose 3.2×, and tier-1 tickets fell by a third across 38,000+ managed handhelds.

07

What I learned

Customer quotes moved the roadmap more than any deck I made. I now put a customer voice in every roadmap review.
