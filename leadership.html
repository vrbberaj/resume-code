Leadership philosophy

Four things I believe about leading design

01

Clarity before pixels

Zero-waste scoping: every initiative starts with a 1-page executive brief defining the user problem, business ROI metric, technical constraints, and definition of done before designers touch Figma.

02

Delegate outcomes, not tasks

Autonomous squad leadership: I empower designers with ownership of measurable problem spaces and definition of done, staying close through rigorous design critique rather than micro-control.

03

Engineering is a design partner

Sprint zero velocity: designers pair directly with engineering leads from sprint zero. Shared design tokens, shared live demos, and shared velocity metrics eliminate handoff churn and code debt.

04

Evidence over opinion

Empirical decision architecture: usability scores, contextual research, production telemetry, and business ROI settle architectural debates—never corporate hierarchy or subjective opinion.

Hiring and growing teams

How I hire

A consistent process, agreed with product and engineering before the first interview.

01

Scorecard

Skills and behaviours agreed with the product and engineering leads before the role opens.

02

Portfolio review

I look for decisions and trade-offs, not polish.

03

Live framing

A 60-minute session where we frame a real problem together. No unpaid take-home work.

04

Partner panel

The candidate meets the PM and engineering lead they would work with.

05

Structured debrief

Everyone scores against the scorecard before discussing, to avoid groupthink.

How I grow people

30-60-90 onboarding

A buddy, a first shipped piece of work inside 30 days, and a clear 90-day goal.

Bi-weekly 1:1s

Their agenda first. Career, blockers, then work.

Growth matrix

Four stages: Craft → Logic → Systems → Strategy. We agree the next stage together each quarter.

Team rituals

Weekly critique, a design review gate before dev, monthly show-and-tell, research readouts.

Delegation

How I delegate

I agree the level of ownership with each designer at the start of a project, so nobody has to guess.

Level 1

I decide

Team structure, hiring decisions, design principles, the quality bar.

Level 2

We decide together

Roadmap priorities with product; design-system changes through the council.

Level 3

You decide, keep me posted

Interaction details, research plans, component design.

Level 4

You own it

Day-to-day execution, visual craft, tools and workflow.

In practice: At IBM, a senior designer owned the model diff drawer end to end. I reviewed it at three checkpoints (brief, mid-fi, pre-release) and otherwise stayed out of the way. It shipped on time and became the pattern for both products.
